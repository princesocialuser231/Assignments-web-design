About HTML

History of HTML

HTML (HyperText Markup Language) is the foundational standard for structuring content across the World Wide Web, using tags and attributes to define elements like headings, paragraphs, links, and media. Its history began in 1989 when British scientist Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web at CERN, creating HTML to enable researchers to share and link scientific documents electronically. He officially published the first specification, "HTML Tags," in 1991, featuring just 18 simple elements. As the internet exploded in popularity throughout the 1990s, the language rapidly evolved through versions like HTML 2.0 and HTML 4.01, transitioning from basic academic text formatting to supporting complex multimedia, styling, and forms. Standardized by the W3C and later driven by the WHATWG, the language underwent its most significant transformation with the rollout of HTML5 in 2014. HTML5 introduced modern semantic tags ( header, article), native multimedia support (video, audio), and dynamic graphics capabilities (canvas), cementing HTML not merely as a document-formatting markup, but as the robust application platform powering the interactive, cross-device modern web.
